Lunch in the Ghetto

Judeo-Roman cooking. Rome's old Jewish quarter, by the Portico d'Ottavia ruins. A short taxi from the end of the Fiat tour, 10 minutes' walk to the Pantheon.

Where to eat

  • Piperno. The pick. Dates to the 1860s, classic dining room on a quiet piazza, known for its carciofi alla giudia. Feels like old Rome, not a tourist strip. Reserve ahead.
  • Nonna Betta. Kosher and family-run; Anthony Bourdain praised its fried artichokes. Busier with visitors but reliable.
  • Ba'Ghetto. Judeo-Roman plus some Middle Eastern dishes, on the main street. Backup if Piperno is full.
  • Il Giardino Romano. Kosher, with a garden dining area. Roman classics like carbonara and fried cod alongside Judeo-Roman dishes.

What to order

  • Carciofi alla giudia: a whole artichoke fried twice until it opens like a crisp flower. Peak season is late winter to spring, so October ones may be less impressive.
  • Filetti di baccalà (fried salt-cod fillets) and fiori di zucca (stuffed fried zucchini flowers).
  • A Roman pasta (cacio e pepe or amatriciana) if you want one, but dinner is already pasta-heavy.

Dessert

  • Pasticceria Boccione, the tiny unsigned bakery on the corner of the main street. Get the ricotta and sour-cherry tart. Expect a short line.
  • Kosher places close Friday evening and Saturday for Shabbat. Wednesday is fine.

Customs

Shops & streets

  • Greet on the way in, say goodbye on the way out. Skipping it reads as rude.
  • Don't handle produce or merchandise. Ask, and let the vendor pick.
  • Churches: shoulders and knees covered. Strictly enforced at St. Peter's and the Duomo.
  • Pickpockets work Trevi, the Spanish Steps, the Colosseum, buses and the Metro. Front pockets only.

Coffee & meals

  • Cappuccino is a morning drink. After about 11 AM or after a meal, order un caffè.
  • Standing at the bar is cheaper than sitting. At busy bars, pay at the register first, then hand the receipt to the barista.
  • Meals are long. Nobody rushes you. Ask for the check when you're ready.
  • Coperto is a small per-person cover charge on the bill. It's normal.
  • Tipping isn't expected. Round up or leave a few euros for good service.
  • Water: you'll be asked naturale (still) or frizzante (sparkling). Tap water and Rome's street fountains are safe.
  • Pasta: no knife, no spoon, no parmesan on seafood.

Food

  • Bistecca alla fiorentina is priced by weight, usually a kilo or more, for sharing. Served rare (al sangue); don't ask for well-done.
  • Tuscan bread is unsalted on purpose, meant for salty salumi and sauces.
  • Lampredotto (tripe sandwich from street carts) is the local street food. Schiacciata sandwiches are the lunch staple.
  • House wine (vino della casa) is usually good and cheap.

Rules

  • No eating on church steps or monuments. Picnicking in parts of the center, like Via de' Neri, is fined.
  • ZTL: never drive a rental into the historic center. Cameras fine you automatically, months later.
  • Leather markets by San Lorenzo: haggle at stalls, not in shops.

Day trip: Motor Valley

  • About an hour to Bologna by fast train. From there: Ferrari Museum (Maranello), Lamborghini (Sant'Agata), Pagani (Modena), Ducati (Bologna). Factory tours need advance booking.
